**Three qualifications, all of which matter when quoting this:**

1. **"100k" is a row count, not a case count.** 100,001 rows = one per (case, event) pair over
   24,476 cases. `system_one/HANDOFF.md` specifies stage S3 as a "100,000-case final scale". A
   24,476-case corpus was built instead. Whether that satisfies the S3 gate as written is an open
   decision — see §6.
2. **"Top three" is ambiguous.** The three arms leading the *operating-point leaderboard* (highest
   F1 at the shared false-positive budget) are `falcon3-1b-instruct`, `granite-4.0-micro` and
   `granite-4.0-1b`. **None of those three has an s3 body.** Ranking position (length-controlled
   AUC) and leaderboard position (F1 at the budget) are different questions and do not pick the
   same arms.
3. **Neither rank 1 nor rank 2 is settled across estimators.** The artifact records
   `rank_1_stable` and `rank_2_stable` as false, and position 3 is contested. See
   [`slm_toolcall/ranking/README.md`](slm_toolcall/ranking/README.md).

### Was it explained on the published Space?

**System One: yes.** The s3 stage is the "Production-weighted" corpus (24,476 scenarios, 100,001
decisions, 99.10% benign). The cascade results for OpenJev, Jev and the judge on it are on
`decide.html`, including the finding that at production weighting the small model alone beats
every judge setting.

**SLM cohort: the composition, not the scores, and less than before 2026-09-24.** Since the
five-page restructure (`3c4ba84`), `datasets.html#second` says the second corpus exists, that 6
models were scored on it, and that it shares 0 case ids with s2. It gives the grade composition
(87.33% grade A against 3.90%) and the per-grade separation AUCs for all six. The previous
revision also published the per-arm row counts, prediction digests and the power analysis; those
are no longer on the Space. They remain in `slm_toolcall/artifacts/s3-stats.json` and
`s3-scores-in-scope.json`. Withheld by decision, and held in the verifier's retired-literal list:

- any transfer penalty (s3 oracle best − s3 at the s2-fitted threshold);
- s3 F1 at the s2-fitted threshold, and s3 shipped block-only F1;
- overall s3 raw AUC per arm;
- any generalisation claim in either direction.

The reason is the grade-composition inversion: a difference between the two corpora mixes a
miscalibrated threshold with a changed definition of a positive.

**One finding is computed and published nowhere, and needs a decision:**
`reconciliation.fact_1_auc_and_f1_orderings_invert` in `artifacts/s3-scores-in-scope.json`.
ShieldGemma orders s3 far better than DeBERTa (raw AUC 0.8955 against 0.6335) while their oracle F1
inverts (0.1208 against 0.4565). AUC is rank-based and prevalence-free; F1 at 0.903% prevalence is
dominated by precision. The artifact's own verdict is "CONFIRMED, and it is not a bookkeeping
error".

Two notes on fidelity:

- **`next_chunk.py` was superseded, not just updated.** The previously vendored copy used a
  strictly-descending claim frontier that cannot recover a gap in the middle; the studio restart
  produced exactly that. The version now in the tree claims the highest unclaimed chunk. Both
  behaviours are documented in `09-reshard/README.md`.
- **`stage_backfill.py` diverges from the host by one line, deliberately.** It holds a *synthetic*
  AWS access key id (SecJudge's own published worked example) whose only purpose is to be redacted
  out of staged files. On vendoring the literal was split into two concatenated halves so the
  repository does not carry a credential-shaped token that GitHub push protection would match. The
  value and behaviour are unchanged; `10-archive/README.md` says so at length.

**A second, whole-host pass found more.** The audit above covered five work directories. Hashing
every `.py` / `.sh` / `.go` under `/home/ubuntu`, excluding venvs, caches, other git checkouts and
third-party code, left 191 of our own files in neither the branch nor HF. The ones that drove
