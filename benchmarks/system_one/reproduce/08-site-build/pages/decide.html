What to deploy

{{fig:dec.rows.s2}} policy rows were measured on the Broad comparison and {{fig:dec.rows.s3}} on the Production-weighted corpus, over {{fig:dec.models.s2}} small models. This page is the part a reader deciding what to run actually needs: what each one costs, what it catches, what it lets through, and how much of it lands on a person. Every number is read from an artifact at build time.

The short answer: for OpenJev (weights CC BY-NC 4.0: non-commercial, attribution required, contact authors for commercial use) on the Broad comparison the cheapest two-sided setting is also its best-scoring one, so there is no trade to make — take two-sided @0.30 at {{fig:dec.spend.s2}} of judge spend per 1,000 cases. That does not hold for every model: the panels below give each one’s own optimum. At the benign share of real traffic the judge stops paying for itself at all: for OpenJev, rules → small model with nothing behind it scores {{fig:s3.so.f1}} against {{fig:s3.twosided.f1}} for the full cascade, at zero provider spend.

At production weighting the no-judge policy is measured for {{fig:s3.nojudge.n}} small models, and OpenJev’s is not the strongest of them. Block-only F1 at zero judge spend: {{fig:s3.nojudge.table}}. On the cost-against-quality panel below, {{fig:s3.nojudge.best.model}}’s is the single undominated point on that corpus. Both it and OpenJev ran question format Q2, so that pair carries no question-format confound. The deployment pick does not follow from this panel alone: on real benign coding traffic {{fig:s3.nojudge.best.model}} flags {{fig:tb.q2.jev}} of events against OpenJev’s {{fig:tb.q2.oj}}, and it has the lowest proof-backed recall of the {{fig:ir.models.n}} models run on the proof-backed corpus ({{fig:recall.jev.C7}} against {{fig:recall.openjev.C7}}). Those are on safety and intent.

Price it against your own traffic

Enter your own volume and benign share. The projection uses the measured rates directly and interpolates only inside the range that was run.

This interpolates between four measured settings. The allow threshold was run at 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30 and at nothing in between. A value between two of them is a straight line drawn between the two measurements on either side — it is not a measurement. The benign share is interpolated between the two corpora that were run, {{fig:calc.lo}} and {{fig:calc.hi}} benign, and clamped to that range. The volume you enter is cases, which is the unit every measured rate here is per; the projection never converts from decisions, so no decisions-per-case ratio is applied at any operating point. If you hold a decisions-per-day figure instead, a scorable case averaged {{fig:calc.dpc}} decisions on the Broad comparison and {{fig:calc.dpc3}} at Production weighting, and the two differ by a factor of {{fig:calc.dpcratio}}. Both are per scorable case, which is the basis the rates use; per scenario the Broad figure is {{fig:calc.dpcscen}}, because {{fig:calc.gradec}} grade-C scenarios are not scorable.

{{ui:calculator}}

What this does not model: your own traffic mix, your own tools, retry behaviour, the latency cost of an in-line judge call, or the human cost of a confirm. It prices the judge tier only, at each corpus’s own token count and the one measured provider rate.
