Worked cases

The {{fig:adj.queue}} cases where the four deciders disagreed, sent to an independent model, {{fig:adj.model}}, blinded to all four votes, then sliced into the four matchups that separate the models. Below that, the code-level failures, quoted from the shipping source.

Model choice: OpenJev agrees with the independent adjudicator on {{fig:adj.unsafe.openjev}} of unsafe cases against DiffusionGemma’s {{fig:adj.unsafe.diffgemma}}. On a block-only reading of the two head-to-head slices, the adjudicator hard-blocks {{fig:adj.m1.block}} of the {{fig:adj.m1.n}} cases OpenJev flagged and DiffusionGemma allowed, against {{fig:adj.m1.allow}} allowed, and hard-blocks {{fig:adj.m2.block}} of the {{fig:adj.m2.n}} in the reverse slice against {{fig:adj.m2.allow}} allowed.

What is published, and what is withheld. Every row of the disagreement queue is redistribution: download-only. Published below: truth grade, surface, event count, source dataset name, the four votes and the adjudicator’s verdict — labels and metadata. Withheld: the tool call, the user request, and the adjudicator’s written reason and apparent_task fields. Where a case needs to be legible, it is paired with a synthetic illustration marked as such. The code-level examples are first-party source and are quoted verbatim.

Agreement with the adjudicator

The adjudicator is one model, {{fig:adj.model}}, blinded to the disputants’ votes but not given ground truth. It shares the small models’ permissive bias: it allows {{fig:adj.permissive}} of the cases the corpus grades unsafe, and {{fig:adj.permissive2}} of the unsafe cases some System One backend already allowed. These matchups measure agreement with one independent opinion. Correctness against truth is a different measurement and is not on this page.

Head to head

Four slices of the same disputed cases. Each names who voted what, how many cases fall in the slice, and how the adjudicator ruled.

{{ui:matchups}}

The four matchups.

  • OpenJev over DiffusionGemma. {{fig:adj.m1.n}} unsafe cases where OpenJev flagged and DiffusionGemma allowed. Adjudicator: block {{fig:adj.m1.block}}, allow {{fig:adj.m1.allow}}, confirm {{fig:adj.m1.confirm}}.
  • DiffusionGemma over OpenJev. {{fig:adj.m2.n}} cases, the reverse pattern. Adjudicator: block {{fig:adj.m2.block}}, allow {{fig:adj.m2.allow}}, confirm {{fig:adj.m2.confirm}}. Which way this slice goes depends on the lens. On a block-only reading DiffusionGemma’s extra flags are upheld on {{fig:adj.m2.block}} and overturned on {{fig:adj.m2.allow}}. Counting a confirm as upholding the flag, they are upheld on {{fig:adj.m2.blockconfirm}} against {{fig:adj.m2.allow}} overturned. The block-only lens is the default in every table on this site.
  • The judge over both small models. {{fig:adj.m3.n}} unsafe cases both small models allowed and Gemma 4 escalated. Adjudicator upheld Gemma 4 on {{fig:adj.m3.block}} blocks plus {{fig:adj.m3.confirm}} confirms. That is the measured cost of routing past the judge.
  • Both small models over the judge. {{fig:adj.m4.n}} benign cases both small models allowed and Gemma 4 escalated. Adjudicator allowed {{fig:adj.m4.allow}} of them. This is the over-hedging two-sided routing removes.

An advisory rule confirm capping a later block

Real measurement. The deterministic tier can terminate a case. When its verdict is an advisory confirm, the chain stops there and a later tier that would have blocked never runs.

Source: outputs/s2/policy-reanalysis-realdet.json, compositions.realdet_short_circuit and compositions.realdet_escalate_on_confirm, two-tier OpenJev then Gemma 4.
