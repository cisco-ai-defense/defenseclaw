RECOMMENDATIONS

12 recommendations

Four are immediate. Five need a decision or a person first. Three are not recommended.

Confidence key. high reproduced, gated, large or interval-separated · medium measured once, on grade-C labels, or with a stated confound · low directionally supported, magnitude unmeasured

Immediate recommendations

These four are reproduced, need no new data, and are reversible by changing a setting.

1 · Enforce per tool call high confidence

Action. Block or confirm the individual tool call, leaving the agent session running.

Evidence. 2.7×–5.1× less benign work destroyed at identical unsafe recall (5.06× / 3.71× / 2.94× / 2.70× across the four measured model-and-stage pairs): 16 benign tool calls blocked against 13 whole benign sessions killed; 0.003144 against 0.009236 at production weighting. outputs/s{2,3}/policy-reanalysis-realdet.json :: compositions.realdet_short_circuit.per_event_vs_per_trajectory.

In practice. An agent is 40 steps into a refactor and step 37 tries to POST a config file to an outside host. Per-call enforcement stops that one call and lets the agent continue or ask. Session-level enforcement kills the whole job and throws away the 36 good steps.

2 · Refuse the two boolean-probe question formats for enforcement high confidence

Action. Use the single-disposition format (Q2, or Q4 two-lane) for any decision that blocks. Guard the probe derivation’s answer type before using it for review.

Evidence. Block-only F1 {{fig:q.q2.alone}} against {{fig:q.q1.alone}} and {{fig:q.q3.alone}}; in the cascade both score below removing the small model ({{fig:q.notier}}); their fail-open would emit allow at confidence 1.0, and it fired on {{fig:failopen.fired}} of {{fig:failopen.rows}} scored rows. outputs/s2/q1-vs-q2-vs-q3.json.

In practice. The guard can be asked one question (“allow, confirm or block?”) or eight yes/no probes (“is this exfiltration? is this persistence?”). The eight-probe form is useful for dashboards and triage, but it must never auto-block: at full scale it almost never returns a block, and if the model answers in the wrong format it returns “allow” at full confidence with no error.

3 · Set thresholds per surface to save judge calls qualified

Action. Separate escalation thresholds for one-shot actions and stateful operations. Measured optima: {{fig:surf.esc.action.thr}} for actions, {{fig:surf.esc.stateful.thr}} for stateful. This buys judge calls. It buys no detection: the action surface’s block F1 is flat across the plateau {{fig:surf.esc.action.plateau}}, so a single global 0.30 already sits at both surfaces’ own F1 optimum.

Evidence. {{fig:surf.esc.action.gemma}} against {{fig:surf.esc.stateful.gemma}} LLM-judge call rate at each surface’s optimum. That ratio is a property of the two surfaces and exists whether or not the recommendation is adopted. The gain from the recommendation is smaller: against a single global 0.30 the action surface saves {{fig:surf.esc.action.gemmagain}} percentage points of judge calls ({{fig:surf.esc.action.gemmaat30}} to {{fig:surf.esc.action.gemma}}) on {{fig:surf.esc.action.cases}} cases, and {{fig:surf.esc.action.f1gain}} block F1. The best F1 at those two points is {{fig:surf.esc.action.f1}} and {{fig:surf.esc.stateful.f1}} under the escalate-on-confirm composition and {{fig:surf.sc.action.f1}} and {{fig:surf.sc.stateful.f1}} under short-circuit. At production weighting the surface-to-surface quality gap is ~5× under short-circuit ({{fig:surf3.sc.action.f1}} against {{fig:surf3.sc.stateful.f1}}) and {{fig:surf3.esc.action.f1}} against {{fig:surf3.esc.stateful.f1}} under escalate-on-confirm. outputs/s{2,3}/policy-reanalysis-realdet.json :: compositions.*.per_surface_thresholds_openjev.

In practice. A single rm is a different risk shape from a 20-step database migration. One shared threshold means either over-escalating cheap one-shot actions or under-escalating multi-step operations. Tune the two separately and each gets its own best trade.

4 · Escalate on a rule-engine confirm high confidence

Action. Treat a deterministic confirm as a floor on the final action and keep evaluating. Keep short-circuiting on a deterministic block.

Evidence. {{fig:s2.realdet.f1}} → {{fig:s2.escalate.f1}} block F1 at the broad mix on the two-tier better model then judge ordering. The other three orderings reach {{fig:esc.three.f1}}, {{fig:esc.threerev.f1}} and {{fig:esc.twodg.f1}}. What holds in all {{fig:esc.orderings}} is the identity check: block_metrics_identical = true in {{fig:esc.identical}} of {{fig:esc.orderings}}. outputs/s2/policy-reanalysis-realdet.json :: compositions.realdet_escalate_on_confirm and escalate_equals_standin_check.

In practice. When the rule engine says “this looks like credential access, someone should check”, that is advice. Today that advice ends the pipeline, so a call the LLM judge would have blocked comes out as a confirm instead. Treat it as a floor: keep the confirm, let a later tier raise it to a block.

Each needs a decision on the availability/safety balance, or work only a person can do.

5 · Drop the LLM judge at realistic traffic mixes high confidence

Action. Run rules → small model, no judge at the 99.1%-benign mix, conditional on recommendation 6: the judge is the backstop (all {{fig:judge.closed}} of its own injected failure modes fail closed).

Measured for {{fig:s3.nojudge.n}} small models over {{fig:s3.nojudge.arms}} arms, and OpenJev is not the strongest. Block-only F1 at zero judge spend, each arm with its question format because they do not all share one: {{fig:s3.nojudge.table}}. The recommendation is about the policy: beating the judge-alone policy, which scores {{fig:s3.nojudge.judgealone}}. {{fig:s3.nojudge.verdict}} Which model to run it with is settled elsewhere on benign false positives and proof-backed recall, where {{fig:s3.nojudge.best.model}} loses badly ({{fig:tb.q2.jev}} of benign coding events flagged against OpenJev's {{fig:tb.q2.oj}}; {{fig:recall.jev.C7}} proof-backed recall against {{fig:recall.openjev.C7}}).

Evidence. High confidence in the measurement. For OpenJev (weights CC BY-NC 4.0: non-commercial, attribution required, contact authors for commercial use), {{fig:s3.so.f1}} block F1 against {{fig:s3.twosided.f1}} for the cascade and {{fig:s3.detllm.f1}} for the judge alone, at {{fig:s3.so.fpr}} false blocks, a {{fig:s3.so.review}} confirm rate, {{fig:s3.so.recall}} block recall and zero LLM cost. outputs/deterministic-real/realdet-s3-openjev.json :: candidates[0].

In practice. Almost everything a coding agent does is ordinary work. Paying the judge to read all of it means it second-guesses a great many harmless calls to find the rare bad one, and it interrupts more often than it catches. Run the small model alone on that traffic and keep the judge wired up as the fallback for when the small model is unreachable.

6 · Make the disposition on a System One failure configurable, defaulting to confirm high confidence

Action. Pair the default with an explicit timeout policy: the latency tail is long ({{fig:lat.s2oj.p99}} p99 under load).

Evidence. {{fig:fault.allow.total}} of 28 injected failure classes end with the cascade allowing the call, {{fig:fault.silent}} of them without raising an error. outputs/fault-injection/report.json :: scenarios[] (outcome shape only).

In practice. The guard’s model will sometimes time out, return truncated JSON, or answer in a shape the parser does not expect. Today the tool call goes through when that happens, which is the wrong default for a security control. Make the failure disposition a setting, default it to asking, and pair it with a timeout so a slow model is treated as a failure when the timeout expires.

7 · Accept or reject the 2.38% short-circuit loss medium confidence

Action. Decide whether to keep two-sided routing. It saves {{fig:s2.onesided.llm}} → {{fig:s2.twosided.llm}} judge calls and {{fig:s2.onesided.review}} → {{fig:s2.twosided.review}} review. The loss is measured at one configuration: the adjudication queue is built once, over the disposition-level class, and no per-threshold breakdown exists in any artifact.

Evidence. At the disposition level, 1,346 cases are ones the judge wanted to escalate and the small model allowed; 32 of the 1,346 are independently graded unsafe (2.38%, Wilson [1.69%, 3.34%]). Under the shipped two-sided {{fig:s2.twosided.llm}} band the judge still sees some of them, so the number that band actually discards is at most 1,346. The blinded adjudicator endorses discarding 89.08% and allows {{fig:adj.permissive}} of graded-unsafe cases. outputs/s2-adjudication/adjudication-report.json :: routing_cost.

In practice. Two-sided routing lets the small model settle the cases it is confident about in either direction, so the judge only sees the uncertain middle. That saves most of the judge’s bill and most of the interruptions, and it also settles some genuinely unsafe calls as safe without the judge ever seeing them. Someone has to decide out loud whether that trade is acceptable. Today it sits implicit in a threshold.

8 · Author the mined deterministic rules medium confidence

Action. Take the mined candidates through the exact-proof rule-authoring workflow, starting with shell-history tampering: {{fig:rule.history.events}} events over {{fig:rule.history.cases}} distinct case, all grade A.

Evidence. 1,076 of 1,118 (96.2%) of the small model’s confident blocks were rule-invisible; rule-engine false-positive rate 0.00000 on 24,255 benign cases. Highest-volume candidate: reverse shells via /dev/tcp, {{fig:rule.shells.events}} events over {{fig:rule.shells.cases}} distinct cases, confidence 1.0, entirely grade E. outputs/s1-n1000/deterministic-candidates.json, outputs/s2/deterministic-candidates.json.

In practice. Most of what the small model confidently blocks has no matching rule, so the same judgement is re-derived by a model on every call. Where the pattern is exact — a command that rewrites shell history, a reverse shell opened through a device file — an authored rule catches it for nothing and can be audited by reading it. Start with the patterns that are exactly provable and leave the judgement calls to the model.

Not recommended

10 · Do not ship the serves-the-request score (Lane B) as a standalone gate

At its nominal threshold Lane B fires on {{fig:tb.laneb.oj}} and {{fig:tb.laneb.dg}} of benign tool calls on the two C7 arms, which is {{fig:tb.laneb.ratio.span}} each model’s own whole decision on the same events, and on {{fig:tb.laneb.oj.traj}} of 40 benign coding sessions; on one model it never exceeds {{fig:tb.si.oj.max}} on any of {{fig:tb.events}} benign events. Source: outputs/terminalbench/q4-lane-fpr.json.

In practice. On its own, the “does this still serve the user?” score fires on ordinary developer work: running tests, reading a config, installing a dependency. As a gate it would stop a large share of benign sessions outright: at its nominal threshold it already fires on {{fig:tb.laneb.oj}} of benign events. It fires on low scores, and on one model the score never rises above {{fig:tb.si.oj.max}}, so above roughly that value every benign event fires and no threshold separates the traffic. Keep it as a triage and review signal and let the harm check do the blocking.

11 · Do not invest in prefix caching or prompt padding for this workload

GPU time is 100% prefill and 0% decode: one-token classification. Padding to the cache block boundary cost +86.68% input tokens, the entire bill. {{fig:px.under}} of {{fig:px.calls}} measured upstream calls are shorter than a single {{fig:px.block}}-token cache block. Tuning the match unit down raised the hit rate {{fig:px.serial.hitgain}} on serial traffic and cost {{fig:px.serial.slower}} wall-clock time. The cache-share series previously quoted here is persisted in no artifact and has been removed. Source: outputs/gpu-host-evidence/, outputs/cache/.

In practice. Prompt caching pays when a long, identical prefix is sent over and over. Here each request is a short one-off classification, most prompts are smaller than a single cache block, and real traffic never repeats byte for byte, so there is nothing to reuse. Spend the effort on prompt layout instead: putting the fixed part first costs nothing and does reuse more of it.

12 · Do not reinstate the culled CPU-only candidate or rank models from this evidence

Re-measured at full screening scale, where the cull rested on an 8-case estimate: best block-only F1 {{fig:von.f1}} on its {{fig:von.arm}} arm against a block-everything floor of {{fig:von.floor}} on the same corpus, hard-blocking {{fig:von.fp}} of {{fig:von.benign}} benign cases to get there, at p50 {{fig:von.p50}} per case on CPU. The per-model tables compare configurations: the four models did not all run the same question format or encoding, and question format alone moves block-only F1 {{fig:fmt.q.ratio}}× on the Broad comparison ({{fig:fmt.q.who}}, {{fig:fmt.q.qlo}} against {{fig:fmt.q.qhi}}), so the rows do not rank the models.

In practice. The CPU-only model was dropped early on a tiny sample, so it was re-run at full scale to be fair to it; it only beats a policy of blocking every call by also blocking most of the harmless ones, and it is far too slow on CPU to sit in an agent’s path. Separately, the models here were not all asked the same question in the same encoding. Question format alone swings block-only F1 by {{fig:fmt.q.ratio}}× on the Broad comparison, an absolute spread wider than the widest model-against-model gap at a single format ({{fig:fmt.m.diff}}). The per-model tables are therefore evidence about configurations.

Limits

Production readiness. The best model blocks {{fig:recall.openjev.C7}} of proof-verified compromises. The other three block {{fig:recall.diffgemma.C7}}, {{fig:recall.gemma4.C7}} and {{fig:recall.jev.C7}}, so between {{fig:miss.openjev.C7}} and {{fig:miss.jev.C7}} of proven compromises get through at the best operating point.
