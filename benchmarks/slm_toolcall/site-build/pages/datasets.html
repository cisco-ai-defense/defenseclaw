The second corpus, and why nothing is compared across the two

A second corpus of {{fig:s3.cases}} cases, {{fig:s3.positives}} of them positive, shares {{fig:heldout.overlap}} case ids with s2, and {{fig:s3.models}} models were scored on it. Its positives are {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}} grade A against s2's {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}}. A change in a model's score between the two would mix a miscalibrated threshold with a changed definition of a positive, so no transfer figure is published in either direction, and no figure elsewhere on this Space uses the second corpus. What is published is the composition and the per-grade separation it explains.

{{chart:grade_slope}} {{chart:grades}}

The two ends make the point. {{fig:mirror.a.model}} reaches {{fig:mirror.a.aucA}} on grade A and {{fig:mirror.a.aucB}} on grade B; {{fig:mirror.b.model}} reaches {{fig:mirror.b.aucA}} and {{fig:mirror.b.aucB}}. A corpus built mostly of one grade therefore ranks models by how they handle that grade.

The {{fig:roster.n}} models

{{fig:roster.general}} general decoders, {{fig:roster.safety}} purpose-built safety classifiers, {{fig:roster.encoder}} trained encoder classifiers and {{fig:roster.control}} untrained controls, {{fig:roster.snapshot.gib}} GiB of weights in all. Repository, pinned revision, parameter count, licence, origin and readout come from the harness's model registry.

{{ui:roster_table}}

Gated downloads

{{fig:roster.gated}} of the {{fig:roster.n}} need a licence-accepted Hugging Face token, across {{fig:roster.groups}} acceptance groups: Gemma, Llama 3.2 and Llama Prompt Guard 2. A 403 means the token is valid and that group is not yet accepted; a 401 means the token itself is not. Community re-uploads of gated weights were not used, because a mirror loses the provenance the licence column records. Anyone repeating the cohort has to accept all three licences. Both Falcon3 models carry the Falcon LLM licence, not Apache-2.0.

{{chart:gating}}

Encoder backbones

Both Prompt Guard 2 sizes are {{fig:pg2.arch}}, with a {{fig:pg2.maxpos}}-token window, so all {{fig:roster.encoder}} trained encoders are checkpoints of one DeBERTa-v2 family. The only other encoder backbone is ModernBERT, and both ModernBERT models are controls. Prompt Guard 2's names understate its size: 22M is {{fig:pg2.22}} parameters and 86M is {{fig:pg2.86}}, because the published figures exclude embeddings.

Qwen3

Qwen3 at 0.6B, 1.7B and 4B was dropped on a non-China provenance constraint, after {{fig:qwen.gib}} GiB of weights had been downloaded and before any GPU time was spent.

Running on a laptop

CPU only, 8 pinned threads. Decoders ran as GGUF Q4_K_M under llama.cpp, encoders as dynamic int8 under transformers. {{fig:lap.published}} models have a measured rows/min figure.

Caveats on these figures

{{ui:caveat_list}}

These figures are archived, not reproducible from vendored code. The CPU measurements were ad-hoc invocations whose scripts were never saved; harness/build_llamacpp.sh builds the toolchain but nothing runs it, and harness/score_arm.py measures no RSS and no CPU latency.
