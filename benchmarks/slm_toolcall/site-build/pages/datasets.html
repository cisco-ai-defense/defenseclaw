The two corpora

Scoring uses s2: {{fig:corpus.cases}} cases, {{fig:corpus.scorable}} scorable, {{fig:corpus.positives}} positive, {{fig:corpus.negatives}} benign, at cases_sha256 {{fig:corpus.sha}}. A second corpus holds {{fig:s3.cases}} cases, {{fig:s3.positives}} positive, {{fig:s3.negatives}} benign, at cases_sha256 {{fig:heldout.sha}}. They share {{fig:heldout.overlap}} case ids. Their positives are built from almost opposite grade mixes: {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}} grade A on s2 against {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}} on the second, so no transfer figure is published from the pair.

How a label was assigned

{{ui:label_provenance}} {{ui:grade_scheme_table}}

Grade A and grade B are scored as positives. Grade C is excluded: the condition it tests is satisfied by a case whose adjudication is unresolved or partial, so a scored decision on one would have no settled truth value to score against. s2 holds {{fig:corpus.gradeC}} grade-C cases, kept for diagnostics and counted in no numerator or denominator here. The second corpus holds none, and neither corpus holds a grade-E case.

Grade A is an unambiguous destructive call and grade B is a judgement call, so the two grades are different evidentiary standards and the mix of them changes what a positive means.

The grade composition of the positives

s2's positives are {{fig:corpus.gradeA}} grade A and {{fig:corpus.gradeB}} grade B, which is {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}} grade A. The second corpus's are {{fig:s3.gradeA}} grade A and {{fig:s3.gradeB}} grade B, which is {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}}. A change in an arm's score between the two conflates threshold miscalibration with a changed definition of a positive.

No transfer figure and no generalisation claim is published in either direction. What is published is the composition itself and the per-grade separation it explains, on Results. This is a corpus-design property and more data of the same kind does not change it.

The length cue on s2

Five scalars read off the request, containing no model at all, separate s2's positives from its benign cases: prompt tokens at AUC {{fig:cue.tokens}}, event count at {{fig:cue.events}}, context_events at {{fig:cue.ctxevents}} and context_bytes at {{fig:cue.ctxbytes}}. Destructive multi-step trajectories in s2 are longer than benign ones, so every raw AUC on s2 carries a length component and every arm carries a length-controlled figure beside its raw one. The decomposition is on Baselines.

On the second corpus the same kind of variable carries nothing: context_bytes reaches {{fig:s3.cue.bytes}}, inside that corpus's chance band of [{{fig:s3.band.lo}}, {{fig:s3.band.hi}}], which follows from a Hanley–McNeil standard error of {{fig:s3.band.se}} at AUC 0.5. context_events reaches {{fig:s3.cue.events}}, below that band. Both fields are corpus properties and are byte-identical across every arm that scored it. The grade A share of its positives is {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}}, so this is a statement about its length fields and not a comparison of the two corpora's difficulty.

How the second corpus relates to s2

The artifacts record three relations between them and no more. They share {{fig:heldout.overlap}} case ids. The second corpus carries {{fig:s3.negatives}} benign cases against s2's {{fig:corpus.negatives}}, and its prevalence is lower by a factor of {{fig:corpus.prev.ratio}}. Its positives are {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}} grade A against s2's {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}}.

The artifacts do not record a mechanism for that inversion in the grade mix, and none is asserted here. What is asserted is the consequence: a score measured on one of these corpora and a score measured on the other are answers to different questions, so the pair supports no transfer figure in either direction.
