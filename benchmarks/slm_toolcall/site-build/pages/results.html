Why this estimator

Three reasons, none of them a count of how many estimators agree. The ranking artifact evaluates {{fig:est.schemes}} schemes and {{fig:est.unwfam}} of them are unweighted means differing only in how the bins are cut, so they share one defect and are not independent witnesses.

  • What it estimates. Pooling is the Mann-Whitney statistic over within-stratum pairs: the raw AUC with the length-confounded pairs deleted and nothing else changed. The unweighted mean estimates a different thing, the same probability averaged with equal weight per length band regardless of how many attacks fall in that band.
  • Weight against evidence. The thinnest quintile holds {{fig:est.q0.pos}} of {{fig:corpus.positives}} positives, {{fig:est.q0.share}} of the evidence. The unweighted mean gives it {{fig:est.q0.wunw}} of the weight, {{fig:est.overweight}}× its share. Pair weighting gives it {{fig:est.q0.wpool}}.
  • Variance. Per-bin Hanley–McNeil at AUC 0.5, a function of bin composition alone, puts the unweighted mean's standard error at {{fig:est.seratio}}× the pooled estimator's, on every candidate arm. The number of arms where it is not worse is {{fig:est.seworse}}.

Position 3 under the four estimators reported here: {{fig:est.rank3.pooled}} pooled, {{fig:est.rank3.unw}} unweighted over all bins, {{fig:est.rank3.ge10}} unweighted over bins with 10 or more positives, and {{fig:est.rank3.ge25}} over bins with 25 or more. As the thin bins are dropped the unweighted mean walks toward the pooled answer. Ranks 1 and 2 are contradicted by one scheme of the {{fig:est.schemes}}, {{fig:est.disagree1}}, whose bins move with each arm's own truncation; rank 3 by {{fig:est.disagree3}}.

Where the published estimator is fragile

{{ui:sparse_bin_note}}

The two corpora do not measure the same thing

A held-out corpus exists, disjoint from s2 with zero case-id overlap, and {{fig:s3.arms}} arms have been scored on it. It is not used here as a generalisation test, and no transfer figure is published: its positives are built from almost the opposite grade mix.

{{ui:corpus_design_note}}

Grade A is an unambiguous destructive call; grade B is a judgement call. s2's positives are {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}} grade A. The held-out corpus's are {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}}. So a change in an arm's score between the two conflates threshold miscalibration with a changed definition of a positive, and a difference between them cannot be read as generalisation in either direction.

{{chart:grades}}

Because the held-out corpus is {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}} grade A, position on it is driven by grade-A separation, and the two extremes of that make the point. {{fig:mirror.a.arm}} reaches {{fig:mirror.a.aucA}} on grade A against {{fig:mirror.a.aucB}} on grade B, from s2 rank {{fig:mirror.a.rank}} on a corpus that is {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}} grade A. {{fig:mirror.b.arm}} is the other end at {{fig:mirror.b.aucA}} on grade A against {{fig:mirror.b.aucB}} on grade B, so it is anti-predictive on exactly the class the held-out corpus is made of, from s2 rank {{fig:mirror.b.rank}}.

Neither of those is evidence about generalisation. Both are evidence that the two corpora measure different things. This is a corpus-design finding and more data of the same kind does not fix it.

The 512-token window

The cap is min(token_budget, max_position_embeddings − 2), which is {{fig:trunc.cap}}. {{fig:trunc.rows}} of {{fig:trunc.rowstotal}} rows exceeded it ({{fig:trunc.rowspct}}); within the scored set {{fig:trunc.scoredrows}} of {{fig:trunc.scoredrowstotal}} rows ({{fig:trunc.scoredrowspct}}) and {{fig:trunc.cases}} of {{fig:corpus.scorable}} cases ({{fig:trunc.casespct}}) carry at least one truncated event. {{fig:trunc.allcases}} cases have every event truncated. A row counted shrunk had its event state cut repeatedly to 82% of its length until it fit, so it lost context the model never saw.

Truncation does not track the arm's errors. AUC inside the truncated stratum is {{fig:trunc.auc}} against {{fig:trunc.aucun}} untruncated, and F1 at the oracle threshold is {{fig:trunc.f1}} against {{fig:trunc.f1un}}. Recall and FPR do differ across the strata, and positive prevalence differs {{fig:trunc.prevratio}}× between them. The mechanism is consistent with build_ids: the shrink trims event history while preserving the decision-relevant current tool call.

The per-row truncated boolean is a different thing: a corpus-level request-build flag, identical at {{fig:trunc.flagrows}} rows in all scored arms including the 16k-context controls.

Two-class arms

All {{fig:roster.scored}} scored arms emit one scalar per event. Three consequences are recorded as facts about those arms.

  • Definitions A and B coincide, because both reduce to the maximum over events for a single monotone scalar. Their AUC carries no definition label.
  • P(block) + P(confirm) and P(block) − P(confirm) are inapplicable: with no confirm class they do not exist, so neither is computed and neither gets a zero substituted for it. The second is also the variable that inverted below chance out of sample.
  • Any-intervention F1 equals block-only F1 by construction, and is reported as an identity.
