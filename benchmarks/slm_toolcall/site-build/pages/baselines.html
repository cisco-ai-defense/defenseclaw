BASELINES

The two baselines

The corpus carries a trivial operating point worth block-only F1 {{fig:floor.f1}} and a length signal worth AUC {{fig:cue.tokens}}, both with no model. Every F1 and every AUC here sits against those two.

The trivial floor

Blocking every case scores block-only F1 {{fig:floor.f1}} at {{fig:corpus.prevalence}} prevalence: tp {{fig:floor.tp}}, fp {{fig:floor.fp}}, fn {{fig:floor.fn}}, tn {{fig:floor.tn}}, precision {{fig:floor.precision}}, recall {{fig:floor.recall}}, block FPR {{fig:floor.fpr}}. Allowing every case scores {{fig:floor.allow.f1}}.

A false-positive rate of {{fig:floor.fpr}} fails any deployment gate, so this is not a claim that trivial blocking competes with a model. It is a claim about what an F1 below the floor tells you: the operating point is broken, and the figure says nothing about whether the model can discriminate.

{{fig:arms.under.floor}} of the {{fig:arms.shipped.cands}} candidates with a shipped decision score below the floor at that decision, and {{fig:arms.zero.f1}} score exactly zero there, blocking no positive case at all. {{fig:top.f1.arm}} has the highest shipped F1 at {{fig:top.f1}}. The floor sits in the same table as every shipped block-only F1, on Operating point.

At each arm's own argmax the picture changes: all {{fig:oracle.clear.floor}} arms clear the floor, the lowest being {{fig:oracle.min.arm}} at {{fig:oracle.min.f1}}. Those figures are in-sample upper bounds and are kept in a separate table.

The second corpus has its own floor. Blocking every case there scores {{fig:heldout.floor.f1}} at prevalence {{fig:s3.prevalence}}; its positives are {{fig:s3.gradeA.share}} grade A against s2's {{fig:s2.gradeA.share}}, so the two floors are properties of two different corpora and are not compared.

{{chart:floor}}

Argmax F1 is a calibration diagnostic here. An in-sample best F1 found by sweeping thresholds on the same rows it is scored on is an oracle upper bound, and is labelled as one wherever it appears.

The length cue

Five scalars read off the request, containing no model, reach AUC {{fig:cue.tokens}} (prompt tokens, max over events), {{fig:cue.events}} (event count), {{fig:cue.ctxevents}} (context_events) and {{fig:cue.ctxbytes}} (context_bytes). Destructive multi-step trajectories in this corpus are longer than benign ones, so this is a property of the data-generating process and not a labelling artifact.

{{chart:cues}}

Every arm's headline AUC therefore contains a length component. Each arm carries a length-controlled AUC beside its raw AUC: the corpus is stratified into five prompt-length quintiles on natural prompt tokens, a corpus property identical for every arm, and the within-quintile AUCs are pooled with each bin weighted by the positive-benign comparisons it holds. That is the {{fig:est.published}} estimator, named in {{fig:est.source}}. An earlier revision averaged the five quintiles unweighted and that estimator is retracted; why.

The pure length counter falls from {{fig:cue.tokens}} to {{fig:cue.tokens.controlled}} under that stratification.

The chance band

For {{fig:corpus.positives}} positives and {{fig:corpus.negatives}} negatives the Hanley–McNeil standard error at AUC 0.5 is {{fig:band.se}}, so the 95% chance interval is [{{fig:band.lo}}, {{fig:band.hi}}]. It is shaded on every AUC chart here.

Both MLM controls land inside or below that band under length control: control-modernbert-base at {{fig:cb.controlled}} and control-modernbert-large at {{fig:cl.controlled}}. Raw figures are {{fig:cb.auc}} and {{fig:cl.auc}}; the decomposition is on Results.
