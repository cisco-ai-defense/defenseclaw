Two ways in: step through a six-screen wizard and we'll assemble a complete policy (**Quick
Start**), or edit the policy surfaces supported by the creator section by section (**Playground**).
Both tabs share the same draft policy, so you can interview-then-tweak. The browser preview evaluates
the OPA/Rego policy domains; the creator separately inventories deterministic detector coverage that
is compiled into DefenseClaw.

<Callout type="info" title="No data leaves the page">
The wizard ships compiled WebAssembly copies of the bundled Rego (admission and
guardrail). OPA evaluation happens entirely in your browser — your draft policy and the
inputs you test against are never uploaded. Saved progress lives in `localStorage`. The Live Test
does not execute ActionFacts parsers, CEL, regex, YARA, or bounded tool-call chains.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn" title="Firewall and audit choices are planning notes">
`defenseclaw policy activate` does not apply the Creator's firewall or audit
choices. They are excluded from the generated policy and install script. Configure
host egress controls and audit retention separately, and verify them on the host
before relying on those choices.
</Callout>

## Current creator coverage

The creator loads its editable rule catalog from the bundled profiles. Balanced/default exposes 243 declared rules (239 enabled), Permissive
243 (235 enabled), and Strict 255 (all enabled). The recipe picker exposes every rule family. For
CEL and tool-call-only counts, see the
[current rule inventory](/docs/policies/deterministic-detection#current-rule-inventory).

The **Deterministic coverage** Playground section is intentionally an inventory rather than an
editor. It shows six ActionFacts capability groups, 26 fixed bounded chains (four
enforcement-capable and 22 alert-only), ten alert-only MCP-description YARA signatures, five
selectable high-assurance packs, and the staged SSH integrity contract. Selectable packs can be
layered directly into the custom rule pack. For the proof model and the complete rule inventory, see
[Deterministic detection reference](/docs/policies/deterministic-detection).

## Power features

These small affordances exist on top of the two main tabs to shorten the most common loops:

| Feature | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Live preview drawer** | Quick Start (every step except Review) | Floating bottom-right pane that evaluates the in-progress admission or guardrail Rego policy against canned scenarios. |
| **Custom test input** | Live Test pane (in the drawer or on the Review step) | Switch the OPA/Rego input source from "canned scenario" to "custom input" and paste your own JSON. Per-domain drafts persist to `localStorage`. Re-evaluates 250 ms after you stop typing. |
| **`⌘K` / `Ctrl-K` palette** | Playground | Fuzzy-search every section and knob ("hilt severity", "webhook secret", "block threshold"). Selecting a result expands the matching section, scrolls to it, and flashes a brief outline. |
| **Syntax-highlighted Rego editor** | Playground → Custom Rego | Zero-dependency editor: transparent textarea over a tokenized overlay. Tab inserts 2 spaces, Shift-Tab dedents. Highlighting is presentation-only — `opa check` runs on your host at install time. |
| **Copy share link** | Quick Start Review + Playground Review | Encodes the current policy into the URL fragment as `gzip + base64url`, copies the link to your clipboard. The fragment never leaves your browser as a network request, but the link itself contains the policy — share it like you would the YAML. |

When someone opens a share link, the page decodes the fragment, prompts before replacing any
in-progress draft, and silently strips the hash. If the payload fails any check (wrong version,
larger than 1 MB after decompression, doesn't look like a policy, malformed gzip) the page shows a
yellow banner explaining which check failed instead of clobbering your draft.

## How the Quick Start works

<Steps>

<Step>

**Step 1 — Posture.** Picks the base preset (Permissive / Balanced / Strict). The Balanced default is
the sensible production starting point; Permissive is for shadow-mode evaluation (only CRITICAL
findings block installs); Strict is for regulated workloads where false-positive cost is acceptable.

</Step>

<Step>

**Step 2 — What to block.** Ten cards grouped under five headers (Data leaks, Network exfiltration,
Code execution, LLM-layer attacks, Multi-step attack patterns). Multi-select. Each card lists the
bundled rule IDs and guardrail patterns it enables, plus firewall destinations to plan separately, with a link to the
[regex cookbook](/docs/policies/regex-cookbook). The multi-step card describes the two session
correlator patterns, which are compiled in and always run, so it adds nothing to the export.

</Step>

<Step>

**Step 3 — What to allow.** Two cards: first-party plugins, which skip admission scans through
`first_party_allow_list`, and internal domains to plan separately (the generated policy does not allow them through the firewall). Free-form text inputs
let you add your own globs and domains. To tune LLM-judge noise, use the Suppressions section in
the Playground and the [suppression cookbook](/docs/policies/suppression-cookbook).

</Step>

<Step>

**Step 4 — Response.** Pick how aggressive enforcement should be: log silently, alert on HIGH+, ask
first (human approval, HITL) on MEDIUM+, or hard block on MEDIUM+. Drives guardrail mode,
thresholds, HITL configuration (the `guardrail.hilt` key), and the skill\_actions matrix.

</Step>

<Step>

**Step 5 — Sinks.** Plan local audit and stdout settings, and configure Slack or generic webhooks.
For Splunk HEC, use [setup splunk](/docs/observability/splunk) after installing the policy;
a generic webhook does not send the HEC Authorization header. Secrets are referenced by env var name only.

</Step>

<Step>

**Step 6 — Review.** Inline policy summary plus the OPA/Rego-only Live Test pane: pick a canned scenario (a
CRITICAL secret in a skill, an MCP install with a sketchy domain, a quarantined plugin), see the
verdict (`blocked` / `allowed` / `quarantined`), and tweak earlier steps until it matches intent.
Then **Open in Playground →** hands you off to the full editor with everything pre-filled. The
Review section there renders every YAML file; copy them into
`~/.defenseclaw/policies/` (or run the bundled install script) and activate the policy, which
writes it into `config.yaml`:

```bash
defenseclaw policy activate my-policy
```

`policy activate` copies the admission policy, guardrail mode, levels, and HITL settings into `config.yaml`; it does not
change the rule pack. The rule pack the creator wrote to
`~/.defenseclaw/policies/guardrail/my-policy/` (rules, suppressions, sensitive tools and judges)
isn't enforced until you validate it and switch to it. `use-pack` pins the pack's digest in
`config.yaml`, and a running gateway loads the pack on its next reload without a restart:

```bash
defenseclaw guardrail validate-pack ~/.defenseclaw/policies/guardrail/my-policy
defenseclaw guardrail use-pack my-policy
```

The install script prints these two commands when it finishes. Try the whole flow in a
throwaway home first with [Verify a policy locally](/docs/policies/verify-locally).

</Step>

</Steps>

## What lives behind each Playground section

The **Backed by** column names the keys in the exported policy file. `policy activate` copies them into
`config.yaml` (for example `guardrail.block_threshold` becomes `guardrail.block_at`); see
[Policies, Layer 1](/docs/policies#layer-1--admission-policy-and-the-opa-engine) for the mapping.

| Section          | Backed by                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Basics           | `name`, `description`, `basedOn` keys in the top-level policy YAML.                                                                    |
| Severity matrix  | `skill_actions.<sev>` (runtime/file/install) plus per-type `scanner_overrides.<type>.<sev>`; activation writes them to `admission.defaults`, `admission.skill` and `admission.<type>.actions`. |
| Admission        | `admission.scan_on_install`, `admission.allow_list_bypass_scan`, and `first_party_allow_list` entries.                                 |
| Guardrail        | `guardrail.mode`, `guardrail.block_threshold` / `alert_threshold` (activation writes `guardrail.block_at` / `alert_at`), `guardrail.hilt`, Cisco trust level. Activation copies the mode, levels, HITL settings, and trust level. The `patterns` and `severity_mappings` it still exports are not read by the gateway since `config_version: 9`; editing them changes nothing. |
| Rule pack        | Per-file regex rules and CEL candidate expressions, including `tool_call_only` scoping (`policies/guardrail/<pack>/rules/<filename>.yaml`). |
| Suppressions     | The three layers: `pre_judge_strips`, `finding_suppressions`, `tool_suppressions`.                                                     |
| Sensitive tools  | `sensitive_tools` array — per-tool result inspection and judge-result toggles.                                                         |
| LLM judges       | `judges.<name>` (pii / injection / tool-injection / exfil) — system prompts and category mappings.                                     |
| Session correlator (Layer 5) | Read-only view of the two post-event correlator patterns compiled into the gateway. They always run and aren't exported. These are distinct from the 26 fixed ActionFacts chains. |
| Deterministic coverage | Read-only inventory of code-owned ActionFacts capabilities, the 26 bounded tool-call chains, and the ten MCP-description YARA signatures; also applies selectable high-assurance rule packs to the custom pack. |
| Firewall         | Planning notes only: `firewall.default_action`, `blocked_destinations`, `allowed_domains`, `allowed_ports` are excluded from export. |
| Webhooks         | `webhooks` array — Slack / Webex / PagerDuty / generic, signed via env-var secrets. Splunk HEC uses an observability destination configured separately.                                                    |
| Watch (rescan)   | `watch.rescan_enabled`, `watch.rescan_interval_min`.                                                                                    |
| Enforcement      | `enforcement.max_enforcement_delay_seconds` is exported but not read by the gateway since `config_version: 9`.                          |
| Audit            | Planning notes only: `audit.log_all_actions`, `audit.log_scan_results`, `audit.retention_days` are excluded from export. |
| Scanner profiles | `scanners.<scanner>` profile selection (codeguard / plugin-scanner / skill-scanner).                                                   |
| Cisco AI Defense (optional · enterprise) | `cisco_ai_defense.enabled` / `endpoint` / `api_key_env` / `scan_hook_surface` — the optional remote lane read by `internal/config`. |
| Custom Rego      | Per-snippet `package defenseclaw.custom.<name>` modules emitted into `policies/rego/custom-<name>.rego`.                               |
| Review & export  | Renders every YAML file the engine needs + a copy-pasteable bash install script that activates the policy.                             |

## Limits to know about

- **Live Test is OPA/Rego-only.** It evaluates the two bundled WASM policy domains, admission
  and guardrail, with the policy as `input.admission` and `input.thresholds`
  the way the gateway compiles it from `config.yaml`. It does not run the Go ActionFacts parser, compile
  CEL, execute RE2 rules, scan MCP descriptions with YARA, or replay bounded chains. Use the exported
  policy on a DefenseClaw host and run the repository test/benchmark harness for those lanes.
- **Fixed deterministic detectors are read-only.** The 26 bounded chains and ten YARA signatures
  shown by the creator are repository-owned runtime/scanner capabilities, not arbitrary policy
  programs. The creator can display their coverage and the active posture, but it does not rewrite
  their implementations. The two Layer-5 session correlator patterns are compiled in as well:
  the creator shows them but can't change them, and a rule pack can't carry its own.
- **CEL does not grant block authority.** A custom CEL expression is a bounded detection selector
  evaluated at an authenticated tool-call boundary. A match cannot make itself enforcement-safe.
  Blocking still requires an eligible synchronous connector, the active profile's disposition, and
  a complete code-owned proof pinned to the same rule ID. Unsupported, ambiguous, conditional, or
  over-budget facts remain detection-only. Browser validation is preflight only; final CEL
  compilation and admission happen on the DefenseClaw host.
- **Rego compilation is offline.** The wizard exports the policy YAML (which `policy activate` writes
  into `config.yaml`) and any custom snippets you author. It does *not* recompile the shipped Rego — for that you still
  need `opa build` on the host. The lint in the Custom Rego section catches the obvious shape
  mistakes (`package` header, balanced braces, `:=` vs `=`).
- **Regex parity.** The browser uses V8 regex; the engine uses Go's `regexp` (RE2). The validator
  flags RE2 incompatibilities (lookaround, backrefs) inline so you catch them before exporting.
  Inline flag groups (`(?i)`, `(?ims)`) are translated to JS flags transparently so bundled rules
  don't false-error.
- **Pre-existing rule packs.** When you switch the base preset, the wizard reloads the bundled
  rule pack so you can see (and edit) the patterns that ship with that pack.
